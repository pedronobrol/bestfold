# World-Record and High-Performance Paper Airplanes: Records, Designs, Principles

(Research date: 2026-11-11. Several popular articles from 2012-2023 still call Suzanne or Toda's planes "current" record holders. They are out of date; see Q1.)

## Q1. Current Guinness records (as of late 2026) and the exact rules

### Takeaway
**Distance:** the current record is **98.43 m (322 ft 11 in)**. Liu Liwen (China) threw it on 28 Dec 2025 in Shanghai, with a team of five other teenagers. The plane is called "Research."
**Time aloft:** the current record is **31.2 s**. The same Chinese team set it on 11 Feb 2026 in Kunshan, beating Takuo Toda's 29.2 s from 19 Dec 2010.
**Rules:** distance attempts must be indoors, use one A4 sheet under 100 gsm, and have no cuts, tears or glue. One clear tape piece of 25 x 30 mm is allowed. The thrower may run up to 3.04 m but must launch from behind a line. A 2026 report says duration rules allow cuts but not splices, with the same 2.5 x 3 cm tape allowance.

**Rules:**
- GWR distance rules: "Flown indoors in a closed-air environment." The plane must be "constructed from only one sheet of A4 that must weigh less than 100 gsm." The thrower "may walk or run up to 10 ft (3.04 m)," but must launch from a static position behind a designated line — [GWR news](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2026/5/evolution-of-the-paper-plane-flight-and-how-far-its-actually-possible-to-throw-one)
- Fold'N Fly's summary of distance rules: maximum A4 and 100 g/m². "The paper airplane must be folded without any glue, tears or cuts." **One clear tape piece of 25 mm x 30 mm** is allowed. Throw indoors behind a line; a crossed foot is disqualified. Distance is measured from the line to the first ground contact — [Fold'N Fly](https://www.foldnfly.com/lounge/world-record-paper-airplanes.php)
- Duration rules: indoors, standard A4 sheet. "The timer starts when the airplane is released and stops when it touches any other object" — [Fold'N Fly](https://www.foldnfly.com/lounge/world-record-paper-airplanes.php)
- 2026 duration attempt, as reported: no larger than A4; "may be cut but not spliced"; tape limited to 2.5 x 3 cm; indoors with judges — [Oddity Central](https://www.odditycentral.com/news/teen-paper-plane-enthusiasts-break-2-long-standing-world-records-in-under-two-months.html)
- Guinness defines a paper aircraft as a sheet of paper folded so it flies when thrown, with clear recognisable wings. Teams use paper at the weight limit because heavier paper carries more momentum at release (Jensen) — [UPI 2023](https://www.upi.com/Odd_News/2023/03/01/Guinness-World-Records-farthest-flight-paper-aircraft/6171677705180/); [foldnfly Mach 5 article (search summary)](https://www.foldnfly.com/lounge/paper-plane-distance-world-record.php)

### Inferences
- An optimizer should use a **210 x 297 mm sheet** with areal density up to about **100 g/m²**, giving a mass of at most about **6.2 g**. It should allow folds only, with tape as a small mass/stiffness term of at most 7.5 cm² (25 x 30 mm, which may be cut into strips; see Suzanne). For duration, cuts can be optional.
- Collins's 2012 record (Suzanne) used US Letter (8.5 x 11 in) per the Make tutorial, and current rules say A4. The rule wording or enforcement may have changed, or Letter fits within "max A4" in some readings. This needs confirmation.
- Distances have risen about 42% since 2012 (69 m to 98 m), and duration about 7% since 2010. Distance appears much more sensitive to throw power and launch technique than duration does.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Guinness's official guideline document, which is given only to applicants. The tape allowance and the cut rule for the duration category come from secondary sources and may differ.
- The 2025 and 2026 Chinese record designs ("Research" and the duration planes) are not publicly described in any source I found.
- I found no authoritative statement on whether US Letter is still accepted.

## Q2. Fold-by-fold structure of the record and well-known designs

### Takeaway
**Suzanne (Collins)** is a wide-span glider with a flat, delta-like wing. It is folded from a sheet with diagonal reference creases, a double-layer nose, and a "pull-down" wing fold. Sixteen tiny tape strips (cut from the allowed 25 x 30 mm piece) lock its layers, and its dihedral varies along the span (165° at the nose, 155° mid-wing).
**Sky King (Toda)** is a fold-only origami glider: reverse-folded corners, a diamond nose lock, and folded elevators.
**Mach 5** and **Cheetah** are only partially documented; their teams have kept details secret.

### Cited Findings
**Suzanne (John Collins), from the Make: tutorial written with Collins:**
1. Diagonal fold: bring the top edge to the left side. Crease and unfold.
2. Diagonal fold the opposite way. Crease and unfold. The two creases form an X near the top.
3. Bring the left edge to the first diagonal crease and the right edge to the other (narrows the top into a point).
4. Fold the top down across the point where the diagonal creases cross.
5. Fold the two top corners down to meet at the center line, along existing creases.
6. Fold the plane in half with a mountain fold (flip, then fold).
7. Rotate a quarter turn so the center crease (keel) faces down.
8. Wing folds: align each wing's creased edge with the center crease without folding yet. Pull the wing down until the small white triangle disappears, then fold. Match the other wing.
- **Tape:** cut 30 mm strips, three about 2.25 mm wide and seven about 1.5 mm wide, and apply **16 strips** in a numbered order. Strips 3, 4, 5, 15 and 16 are the wider ones. Exact placement is shown only in diagrams.
- **Dihedral:** set with a cardboard protractor gauge to **165° at the nose and 155° at mid-wing**. The variable dihedral "optimize[s] lift-to-drag across a range of speeds."
- **Trim:** turns are corrected at the trailing edge. "Faster throws need adjustments closer to the nose." If it veers right at launch, bend the left leading edge down slightly at the nose.
- **Throw:** hold it at the thickest part, where most layers meet. Keep the wings level and accelerate smoothly. Get it flying straight first, then build up to a fast throw.
- All of the above — [Make: "World's Best Paper Airplane"](https://makezine.com/projects/worlds-best-paper-airplane/)

**Why Suzanne is a glider, not a dart:**
- Earlier record holders were "ballistic darts" with small fin-like wings used only for direction, launched at 45°, that crashed into the finish. Suzanne launches level, climbs by generating lift, flies the last third of the throw, and lands by gliding and skidding. It took **9 s** to fly the record distance, versus **3 s** for a dart — [Make:](https://makezine.com/projects/worlds-best-paper-airplane/)
- Collins: "Previous distance marks were set by planes that were basically sticks with fins." He spent about 3 years studying aerodynamics and testing designs. The full folding sequence is in *The New World Champion Paper Airplane Book* — [search summary of NowIKnow / Mental Floss / Penguin Random House](https://nowiknow.com/?p=7553)
- Joe Ayoob has reportedly thrown Suzanne more than 240 ft (about 73 m) outside official attempts — [Make:](https://makezine.com/projects/worlds-best-paper-airplane/)

**Mach 5 (Ruble, Jensen, Erickson; 88.31 m):**
- Inspired by hypersonic aircraft, specifically NASA's X-43A. Folding the record plane took more than 20 minutes. The team spent about 6 months optimizing. The record came on the 3rd throw — [Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/science/paper-plane-distance-record); [Fold'N Fly article (search summary)](https://www.foldnfly.com/lounge/paper-plane-distance-world-record.php)
- Design goal: "cover the most distance in the shortest time" (a speed-focused design). Flight time was about 6 s — [Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/science/paper-plane-distance-record); [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Odd_News/2023/03/01/Guinness-World-Records-farthest-flight-paper-aircraft/6171677705180/)
- No authoritative fold sequence was found. A third-party step-by-step exists at [scienceinsights.org](https://scienceinsights.org/how-to-make-the-mach-5-paper-airplane-step-by-step/) but is not verified against the team.

**Cheetah (Chee / Shin / Kim; 77.134 m):**
- Chee designed the folding sequence, and Shin modified it "to improve its flight characteristics" (no specifics given). Shin called demonstrating it publicly "an error in judgment" because someone reverse-engineered it. A prototype tutorial video exists on the Foldable Flight site — [Foldable Flight](https://www.foldableflight.com/post/a-new-world-record-paper-airplane-distance)
- Fold'N Fly mentions a "400 hours running computer simulations" detail, attributed to the linked 2022-team interview. It is unclear which 2022 team this refers to; I believe it is the Korean/Malaysian team but could not confirm — [Fold'N Fly](https://www.foldnfly.com/lounge/world-record-paper-airplanes.php)

**Sky King (Takuo Toda):**
- The record-setting Sky King (27.9 s, 2009) was made from a single uncut sheet with no glue — [Time](https://content.time.com/time/specials/packages/article/0,28804,1934027_1934003_1933991,00.html)
- Elevators are made by folding, not by cutting flaps. The folds include a reverse fold for the corners and a diamond shape at the nose — [WIRED "How to Fold a Sky King" (search summary)](https://rosetta.to/u/wired/how-to-fold-a-sky-king)
- "Hyper Sky King" is a modification of the record-setting model — [PopSci 2012](https://www.popsci.com/technology/article/2012-04/video-how-fold-longest-flying-paper-airplanes/)
- Toda's books *Ultra Long Flight Paper Airplanes* and *Extreme Distance Paper Airplanes* (Tuttle) contain official templates — [NewSouth Books](https://newsouthbooks.com.au/books/ultra-long-flight-paper-airplanes/)
- Toda says to throw it "nearly straight up to get the most height," which "gives the glider a long time to go in circles before reaching the ground" — [Fold'N Fly](https://www.foldnfly.com/lounge/world-record-paper-airplanes.php)

**Ken Blackburn's duration planes:**
- "Remarkably simple," with a **low-aspect-ratio ("short and stubby") rectangular wing**. He "throws it directly upwards at 60mph." A high-aspect-ratio paper wing "could not withstand the launch forces," so he accepts a higher sink rate in exchange for a higher peak altitude, which the source calls "a near-optimal solution" — [Symscape](https://www.symscape.com/blog/paper-airplanes.html)
- Blackburn learned dihedral and sink rates through fold-fly-tweak trial and error — [Air & Space / Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/toy-story-10416004/)

### Inferences
- Two distinct design families matter for the optimizer:
  - **(a) Duration:** a compact, stiff, low-aspect-ratio glider that is thrown about vertically and then transitions to a slow circling glide. Its structure must survive about 27 m/s (60 mph).
  - **(b) Distance:** either a wide-span lifting glider thrown hard and roughly level (Suzanne, 9 s), or a fast, low-drag shape thrown hard at about 40° (Mach 5, 6 s, covering more distance in less time).

  The jump from 69 m to 88 m with a speed-oriented design suggests the current optimum trades some glide for launch energy retention.
- Tape is used as many tiny strips to lock layers (keeping the fuselage and keel tight and stopping layers opening under high-speed loads), not as a structural spar.

### Gaps
- **Nakamura Lock, Phoenix, Harrier:** I did not retrieve sourced fold sequences in this pass. From general knowledge, which is unverified here:
  - Nakamura lock: fold the top corners to the center, fold the tip down, fold the corners to the center again, then fold the small tip up to lock.
  - Harrier: a similar locked-nose glider.
  - Phoenix: a Fold'N Fly design.
  - Verify all three on [foldnfly.com](https://www.foldnfly.com/) before use.
- I found no full fold sequences for Mach 5, Cheetah, "Research," or Blackburn's record plane from primary sources.
- I found no source on whether Toda's 29.2 s plane used tape or cuts. The record page did not state construction.
- I could not reach Ken Blackburn's site (paperplane.org redirects to an unrelated domain), so his own design text was unavailable.

## Q3. Design principles stated by experts

### Takeaway
The experts agree on a CG ahead of the center of lift, achieved through nose layering. They also stress up-elevator trim at the trailing edge, positive dihedral (Suzanne 155-165°), tight taped layers, and symmetry and precision. Trim is done iteratively by test throws. Launch technique is category-specific:
- **Duration:** throw near-vertical, very hard.
- **Distance:** a glider (Suzanne) is thrown level and hard; a fast dart-like design (Mach 5) is thrown about 40° up.

### Cited Findings
- Collins: flight requires a center of gravity just forward of the center of lift — [search summary citing Collins](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/54488/perfect-paper-airplane)
- In a well-designed glider the CG sits ahead of the center of lift, so the nose drops. Folds near the nose concentrate weight forward and stiffen the paper. Small folds at the wing's trailing edge control flight — [Exploratorium](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/exploring/paper/airplanes2.html)
- Suzanne's variable dihedral (165° nose, 155° mid-wing, gauge-set) targets L/D over a range of speeds. Trim at the trailing edge, and closer to the nose for faster throws — [Make:](https://makezine.com/projects/worlds-best-paper-airplane/)
- Collins's book covers dihedral and elevator adjustments as the tuning tools for stability — [WIRED video summary (search)](https://rosetta.to/u/wired/how-to-fold-five-incredible-paper-airplanes-wired)
- Mach 5 launch: "about 40 degrees off the ground ... Once you're aiming that high, you throw as hard as possible." The angle was set using simulations and slow-motion video — [Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/science/paper-plane-distance-record)
- Old dart records were launched at 45° — [Make:](https://makezine.com/projects/worlds-best-paper-airplane/)
- Design iteration: Mach 5 used theorize, fold, throw, compare distance. Blackburn used fold-fly-tweak trial and error. The Chinese team brought 10 candidate designs and relied on on-site "calibration" members — [Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/science/paper-plane-distance-record); [Air & Space](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/toy-story-10416004/); [Shanghai Daily](https://www.citynewsservice.cn/articles/shanghaidaily/news/chinese-teens-set-paper-plane-world-record-nearing-100-meters-png9o7pm)
- Liu Liwen: the result depends on "the synthesis of paper quality, fold structure, aerodynamics, and throwing technique" — [Shanghai Daily](https://www.citynewsservice.cn/articles/shanghaidaily/news/chinese-teens-set-paper-plane-world-record-nearing-100-meters-png9o7pm)
- Guinness: distance "depends heavily on the precision with which it was created"; attempters are "pretty secretive about their folding techniques" — [GWR news](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2026/5/evolution-of-the-paper-plane-flight-and-how-far-its-actually-possible-to-throw-one)
- Small-scale manufacturing variability has a significant effect on flight. Pure CFD misses paper flexibility, which would need fluid-structure interaction — [Symscape](https://www.symscape.com/blog/paper-airplanes.html)
- Heavier paper (up to the limit) gives more momentum at release — [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Odd_News/2023/03/01/Guinness-World-Records-farthest-flight-paper-aircraft/6171677705180/)

### Inferences
- Optimizer design variables should include:
  - nose layer count and overlap (sets CG location and stiffness)
  - spanwise dihedral distribution
  - elevator (trailing-edge reflex) angle
  - wing planform and aspect ratio, constrained by launch-load survivability
  - keel depth
  - tape placement as a constraint on layer separation
  - launch speed and angle as co-optimized parameters, since the optimum airframe depends on launch (level-hard vs about 40° vs vertical)
- A robustness or tolerance analysis (sensitivity to fold error of about 1 mm and about 1°) is warranted given the emphasis on precision.

### Gaps
- No sourced quantitative CG position (% of mean chord) or static margin for any record plane was found.
- No sourced statement on washout or on how Suzanne handles the launch-to-glide transition beyond "launches level, climbs by generating lift."
- The rule that the launch should bank for duration throws (to enter a circling glide) is commonly stated but was not sourced in this pass.

## Q4. Typical performance numbers

### Takeaway
The record distance planes fly 6-9 s:
- Suzanne: 69 m in about 9 s, about 7.7 m/s average
- Mach 5: 88 m in about 6 s, about 14.7 m/s average

The duration record of 31.2 s comes from a hand launch of about 27 m/s (60 mph, Blackburn's figure), roughly vertical. I found no sourced glide ratio or glide speed for these planes.

### Cited Findings
- Suzanne: 9 s for the record flight; dart records: about 3 s — [Make:](https://makezine.com/projects/worlds-best-paper-airplane/)
- Mach 5: about 6 s airborne for 88.31 m — [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Odd_News/2023/03/01/Guinness-World-Records-farthest-flight-paper-aircraft/6171677705180/)
- Blackburn launch: "directly upwards at 60mph" — [Symscape](https://www.symscape.com/blog/paper-airplanes.html)
- Duration records: 15 s (1975) to 31.2 s (2026) — [Fold'N Fly](https://www.foldnfly.com/lounge/world-record-paper-airplanes.php); [GWR JP](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.jp/world-records/longest-time-flying-a-paper-aircraft)

### Inferences
- Average speeds computed from the cited figures: Suzanne about 69.14/9 = 7.7 m/s; Mach 5 about 88.31/6 = 14.7 m/s; Liu's 98.43 m flight time is unknown.
- A duration plane released at about 27 m/s vertically would ballistically reach at most v²/2g, about 37 m, ignoring drag. With heavy drag losses the real apex is much lower, so a large share of the 30 s is the glide descent. This is a sanity bound only.

### Gaps
- No sourced glide ratio (L/D), sink rate, or glide speed was found for any record plane, nor sourced flight times for typical casual planes. Literature values of roughly L/D 4-8 for good paper gliders exist in general knowledge but were not verified here. The aerodynamics-literature researcher should supply these.
- I found no published aerodynamic analysis (CFD or wind tunnel) of Suzanne, Mach 5, or Sky King. The search summary explicitly noted no technical analysis of Suzanne turned up.
