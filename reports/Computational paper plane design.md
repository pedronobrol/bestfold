# Fold, fly and validate the record-breaking paper plane

The best computationally designed no-tape A4 plane will come from a fold engine that is far more expressive and a scoring layer that is far less ad hoc, not from a better optimizer. Your JADE-style differential evolution is a defensible core: success-history DE variants are still near the top of the CEC competitions. The weak points are elsewhere. The genome can only express all-layer diagonal and tip folds, so it cannot reach the reverse folds, locks and layered noses that record designs depend on. The flight model throws away the leading-edge suction that becomes vortex lift on a dart, and it under-predicts drag on slow gliders by about half. Launch speeds are fixed at 12 and 16 m/s, while record throwers reach about 27 m/s. Constraints enter as multiplicative penalties, and the ten one-at-a-time scenarios never perturb two things at once. The current records set the target: **98.43 m for distance and 31.2 s for time aloft, both set in 2025–26 by a Chinese teenage team**. Both categories allow a small piece of tape, so your no-tape rule is stricter than Guinness. The closest like-for-like benchmark is Takuo Toda's **26.1 s with a single uncut, unglued sheet**. Your strongest advantage is that nobody has published a search over real fold sequences with a foldability check: earlier computational work tuned at most five fold parameters. Your largest risk is that the optimizer exploits drag, flex and launch physics the simulator gets wrong, so real throws must enter the loop early and judge whether the simulator ranks designs in the right order.

## Records reward three different flight strategies, and the no-tape rule changes which ones apply

Distance records have risen fastest. Tony Felch's 58.82 m in 1985 stood for decades. Joe Ayoob threw John Collins' "Suzanne" 69.14 m in 2012, a Korean–Malaysian team reached 77.13 m in 2022, Boeing engineers reached **88.31 m with "Mach 5"** in December 2022, and Liu Liwen's team reached **98.43 m with "Research"** in December 2025 ([GWR news](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2026/5/evolution-of-the-paper-plane-flight-and-how-far-its-actually-possible-to-throw-one); [GWR record page](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/farthest-flight-by-a-paper-aircraft)). Duration has moved far less. Blackburn's 27.6 s (1998) gave way to Toda's 29.2 s (2010) and then to the same Chinese team's **31.2 s in February 2026**, where the best individual flights were 31.19 s and 31.25 s and the team brought its "10 best designs" ([GWR JP](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.jp/world-records/longest-time-flying-a-paper-aircraft); [Oddity Central](https://www.odditycentral.com/news/teen-paper-plane-enthusiasts-break-2-long-standing-world-records-in-under-two-months.html)). Distance gained about 42% after 2012 while duration gained about 7% after 2010. That gap suggests launch power and its interaction with the airframe drive distance far more than glide efficiency alone does.

The rules matter for how you set up the problem. Guinness distance attempts use one A4 sheet under 100 gsm, flown indoors with no cuts, tears or glue. **One 25 × 30 mm piece of clear tape is allowed**, and the thrower may run up to 3.04 m before a line ([GWR news](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2026/5/evolution-of-the-paper-plane-flight-and-how-far-its-actually-possible-to-throw-one); [Fold'N Fly](https://www.foldnfly.com/lounge/world-record-paper-airplanes.php)). Reports of the 2026 duration attempt describe cuts as allowed but not splices ([Oddity Central](https://www.odditycentral.com/news/teen-paper-plane-enthusiasts-break-2-long-standing-world-records-in-under-two-months.html)). Your no-tape, no-cut rule is therefore stricter than both record categories. Suzanne used **16 hair-thin tape strips** to keep its layers from opening at launch speed ([Make:](https://makezine.com/projects/worlds-best-paper-airplane/)). Toda's long flights are reported to have used scissors or tape, while his pure-origami flight of **26.1 s** is the cleanest benchmark for your rules ([Make: 2010](https://makezine.com/article/home/fun-games/paper-airplane-flight-time-record-a)). The practical consequence is that, without tape, fold locks and friction-held layers must do the job tape does on record planes. That makes lock modelling a first-order requirement rather than a nicety.

The record designs fall into three families. Pre-2012 distance planes were "basically sticks with fins": darts launched at about 45° that took roughly 3 s to cover the distance. Suzanne instead launches level, climbs on its own lift and glides the final third, taking **9 s for 69 m** with dihedral set at 165° at the nose and 155° mid-wing to hold L/D across a range of speeds ([Make:](https://makezine.com/projects/worlds-best-paper-airplane/)). Mach 5 is a speed design launched at **about 40°**, an angle the team chose with simulation and slow-motion video, "as hard as possible". It covered 88 m in about 6 s, an average of roughly 14.7 m/s ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/science/paper-plane-distance-record); [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Odd_News/2023/03/01/Guinness-World-Records-farthest-flight-paper-aircraft/6171677705180/)). Duration planes are compact, stiff and low in aspect ratio. Blackburn throws "directly upwards at 60 mph" because a high-aspect-ratio paper wing "could not withstand the launch forces" ([Symscape](https://www.symscape.com/blog/paper-airplanes.html)). Toda advises a near-vertical throw so the glider circles down ([Fold'N Fly](https://www.foldnfly.com/lounge/world-record-paper-airplanes.php)), and his Sky King uses folded elevators, reverse-folded corners and a diamond nose lock rather than cuts ([WIRED summary](https://rosetta.to/u/wired/how-to-fold-a-sky-king)).

Across all of them, experts converge on a few principles: a CG just ahead of the centre of lift, set by nose layering; trailing-edge up-elevator trim; positive dihedral; and extreme folding precision. Guinness notes that distance "depends heavily on the precision with which it was created" ([GWR news](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2026/5/evolution-of-the-paper-plane-flight-and-how-far-its-actually-possible-to-throw-one); [Exploratorium](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/exploring/paper/airplanes2.html)). Teams also fold at the weight limit, because heavier paper carries more momentum at release ([UPI](https://www.upi.com/Odd_News/2023/03/01/Guinness-World-Records-farthest-flight-paper-aircraft/6171677705180/)). At 100 gsm an A4 sheet weighs 6.24 g, against 4.99 g at the 80 gsm hard-coded in `src/design.js`. The fold sequences of Mach 5, Cheetah and Research are unpublished, and no wind-tunnel or CFD study of any record plane exists. The optimizer therefore cannot copy the winners and has to rediscover them, and that requires a design space large enough to contain them.

## Some-layers folds plus reverse-fold and lock macros make record-style designs reachable

Your engine's `fold.js` performs only all-layer simple folds. Its genome (`design.js`, 24 genes) allows at most four nose folds, each either a "diag" or a "tip" fold, inside a fixed template. That is enough for darts and simple gliders. It cannot express the moves record designs use: an inside reverse fold, a squash, folding only the top flap over a lock, or interleaving layers across the centre line. The complexity literature shows why you should not fix this by searching over crease patterns. Deciding whether a crease pattern is globally flat-foldable is NP-hard (Bern & Hayes 1996), and simple-foldability of an assigned crease pattern is NP-complete under the one-layer, some-layers and all-layers models alike ([Akitaya et al. 2016](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/SimpleFolds_JCDCGGG2016/paper.pdf); [arXiv:2306.00702](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00702)). Every one of those hardness results concerns the inverse problem, though. A forward generator that applies one operation to a known valid flat state only needs, at each step, polynomial geometry, a stacking rule and a local check. Verifying a facewise layer order takes **O(n²p + …) ≤ O(n³)** time ([Akitaya, Demaine, Ku 2024](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/FlatFolder_OSME2024/paper.pdf)), and a paper airplane has roughly 10–200 faces and a ply of at most about 16. The NP-hardness never comes into play in a forward generator.

The recommended representation keeps your facet-plus-rigid-transform model and adds three pieces of state. The first is an explicit per-cell layer stack: an ordered array of facet ids for each region of the folded arrangement. This is equivalent to the FOLD format's `faceOrders` ([FOLD spec](https://edemaine.github.io/fold/doc/spec.html)) and makes "the top k layers here" an O(1) query. The second is a hinge graph with mountain/valley state. The third is a FOLD exporter, so any candidate can be cross-checked in Jason Ku's MIT-licensed Flat-Folder ([GitHub](https://github.com/origamimagiro/flat-folder)) during development. Rabbit Ear has an importable JS layer solver, but it is GPLv3 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/rabbit-ear)), so borrow from Flat-Folder instead.

On that state, generalise the fold primitive to **SomeLayersFold(line, side, k, mountain/valley)**, where your current fold is the special case k = ∞. Each such fold needs a connectivity closure: any layer hinged to a moving layer off the fold line must move too, or the paper tears. Then add compound macros. An inside or outside reverse fold mirrors the fold line through both layers of a spine-folded flap, flips the spine segment beyond it, and inserts the reversed pair between, or around, the stationary layers. A squash fold can be built as "cut a hinge, do two simple folds, rejoin", following Ida & Takahashi ([arXiv:2201.00536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.00536)). ORIZU's catalogue of diagram "maneuvers", which it applies backwards as graph rewrites, is the right checklist of macros to implement forwards ([SIGGRAPH 2013 poster](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2013-Poster-47-Akitaya_Generating-Folding-Sequences-from-Crease-Patterns-of-Flat-Foldable-Origami.pdf)). Model a Nakamura-style lock as a k = 1 fold of the top flap. Its effect then becomes a derived feature: the set of facets whose free edges are trapped under a folded-over flap. Feed that feature to the structural model as resistance to unfolding. No paper formalises reverse folds or locks on layered states, so this part is engineering synthesis rather than settled theory.

Validate each step locally. After a fold, recheck only the cells touched by moved facets against the Akitaya–Demaine–Ku conditions. Two collinear folded creases ("tacos") must be nested or disjoint in the stack, never interleaved. No face may sit between the two layers of a taco crossing it. Transitivity must hold ([Akitaya, Demaine, Ku 2024](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/FlatFolder_OSME2024/paper.pdf)). When a macro leaves insertion positions ambiguous, Flat-Folder's method of splitting the constraint graph into components and brute-forcing each one stays tiny at airplane ply. With at most about 64 facets, 8 steps and ply 16, the estimated cost is microseconds to a millisecond per step in JS, but nobody has measured that. Snap fold-line parameters to a grid or to rational reference points. Exact folded geometry runs into the open sum-of-square-roots problem, and snapping both avoids sliver facets and makes the design foldable by a person with a ruler.

For the genome, replace the four-slot nose template with a fixed-maximum-length array of K_max = 8–12 slots. Give each slot a categorical operation type (some-layers fold, reverse, squash, lock, no-op), continuous parameters expressed relative to the current geometry (for example, a fraction along an existing edge rather than an absolute coordinate), and a layer selector k. Add a symmetry flag so mirrored pairs are generated by construction. This "decoder" style removes infeasibility at the source instead of rejecting candidates afterwards. No prior work does this. Pteromys optimised unfolded flat gliders ([Autodesk Research](https://www.research.autodesk.com/publications/pteromys-interactive-design-and-optimization-of-free-formed-free-flight-model-airplanes/)), PaperBot searched five fold-and-throw parameters with a real robot ([arXiv 2403.09566](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09566v1)), and EPFL's robot covered about 50 parametric designs ([Sci. Rep. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-31395-0)).

## Drag calibration, vortex lift and launch-load flex dominate simulation error

The measured data are thin but clear enough to calibrate against. For thin flat-plate wings of aspect ratio 2–5 at Re 60–160k, Ananda, Sukumar & Selig measured **CLmax ≈ 0.55–0.7** (nearly independent of AR), stall near **α ≈ 10°** followed by a flat lift plateau to 30°, **CDmin ≈ 0.01–0.02**, no hysteresis, a nose-down moment break at stall, and lift slopes that follow Helmbold theory. Their Oswald factors were strikingly low, **e0 ≈ 0.3–0.8**, falling as aspect ratio rose ([Ananda et al. 2015](https://m-selig.web.engr.illinois.edu/pubs/AnandaSukumarSelig-2015-AeroSciTech-LowReWings.pdf)). Below Re 70k, thin plates are insensitive to Reynolds number and turbulence, and a **5% circular-arc camber** is the best profile ([Laitone 1997](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2Fs003480050128.pdf)). Cambered plates beat flat ones ([Pelletier & Mueller 2000](https://typeset.io/papers/low-reynolds-number-aerodynamics-of-low-aspect-ratio-thin-2jtrp6hdzi)). Curl and camber are therefore worth modelling explicitly, while surface roughness is not.

Two real paper planes give calibration targets. Ng et al.'s A4 dart (AR 1.1, 67° sweep, about 5 g) flew at **4.9 m/s with L/D ≈ 5.0 and CL ≈ 0.17**, trimmed near α ≈ 5°, and stalled only at **α ≈ 30°** in the water tunnel. That large gap between cruise and stall angle explains the dart's tolerance of bad launches ([Ng et al. 2009](https://lactea.ufpr.br/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/On_the_Aerodynamics_of_Paper_Airplanes.pdf)). A Kent-paper record-designer glider (AR ≈ 9.5) measured at Re 1.23×10⁴ gave **CDmin = 0.0685, (L/D)max = 8.82 at α = 2°, minimum sink 0.497 m/s**, and a free-flight speed of 5.04 m/s ([Tokuhiro et al. 2017](https://www.cst.nihon-u.ac.jp/research/gakujutu/61/pdf/K3-57.pdf)). By my reading of `physics.js`, your viscous model (Blasius friction on both surfaces plus `FORM_CD = 0.012`) yields CD0 ≈ 0.020 at Re 10⁵, which matches the dart. At Re 1.2×10⁴ it yields only about 0.036, roughly half the measured glider value. This error matters most. Glide distance scales with L/D, and L/D is set mainly by CD0 and e0, so a 2× CD0 error shifts predicted range by tens of percent. Lift-slope errors mostly just move the trim angle. The optimizer will exploit this error by drifting toward slow, high-aspect-ratio gliders. Make the form-drag term depend on Reynolds number and layer count, then fit it to both measured planes.

The second correction concerns vortex lift. Your code keeps 20% of the leading-edge suction as thrust (`SUCTION = 0.2`) and deletes the rest. Polhamus' analogy says the suction lost at a sharp edge **reappears as a normal force**, CL = Kp·sinα·cos²α + Kv·cosα·sin²α with ΔCD = CL·tanα. NASA describes this as giving "excellent predictions" for sharp-edged deltas over a wide range of α and aspect ratio ([Polhamus TN D-6243](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19710012498/downloads/19710012498.pdf)). Sharp-edged delta lift is nearly independent of Re down to Re 60k ([Ng et al. citing Traub et al.](https://lactea.ufpr.br/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/On_the_Aerodynamics_of_Paper_Airplanes.pdf)). The fix is local. On strips whose leading edge is swept more than about 55–60°, and on side edges where aspect ratio is below about 1.6 (where nonlinear lift appears, [J. Mech.](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-mechanics/article/aerodynamic-investigations-of-lowaspectratio-thin-plate-wings-at-low-reynolds-numbers/9099066D94CDB704D93C86F0752B3427)), rotate the removed in-plane component to act along the panel normal, and set retained thrust to zero. This is the Lamar side-edge extension ([via Ananda et al.](https://m-selig.web.engr.illinois.edu/pubs/AnandaSukumarSelig-2015-AeroSciTech-LowReWings.pdf)). Traub disputes whether unswept edges produce leading-edge vortex lift in steady flow ([Aerospace 2023](https://mdpi.com/2226-4310/10/7/597)), so apply the correction only to tips and swept edges. Your stall blend, which starts at an effective 11° and falls back to a plate stall at 30–45°, is consistent with the data. The rest of the stalled model should follow Ananda's results: a plateau rather than a drop for moderate-AR wings, and a centre of pressure that moves aft to produce a nose-down break.

Structure is the third gap. Your stiffness check evaluates tip deflection under a 1 g glide load only, using E = 3 GPa and n² layer scaling. That is too forgiving exactly where it matters. A flat single-layer half-wing of 0.1 m under about 2.5 Pa wing loading would droop about 7 cm even at 1 g. Dynamic pressure rises from about 22 Pa at 6 m/s to **87 Pa at 12 m/s and 439 Pa at 27 m/s**, which is why Blackburn's high-aspect-ratio paper wings failed at launch ([Symscape](https://www.symscape.com/blog/paper-airplanes.html)). Datasheet bending resistance for 80 gsm copy paper is about MD 110 / CD 55 mN, a **2:1 anisotropy** ([Lomax](https://cdn.lomax.dk/image/upload/dokumenter/1515200_4.pdf)). That implies E_MD ≈ 3.5–4 GPa and E_CD ≈ 1.8–2 GPa, an inference that assumes a 10 mm test span. Measured paper creases act as nonlinear torsional springs carrying about **0.01–0.07 N·m/m** as they open, with large specimen scatter (residual SD 0.29 N·mm over 43 specimens) and no dependence on grain direction ([Pradier et al. 2016](https://hal.science/hal-01276578)). Creases also creep and lose their rest angle ([Jules et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.04892)).

The right model is a quasi-static bar-and-hinge solve on the folded mesh, using Origami Simulator's explicit per-node update ([Ghassaei et al.](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/OrigamiSimulator_Origami7/paper.pdf)) with physical, grain-dependent panel and crease stiffness. Run it at three dynamic-pressure levels (launch, transition, glide) and feed the deformed twist, dihedral and camber back into the vortex lattice. This is justified because a folded paper wing's first bending mode, of order tens of Hz, sits well above the 1–3 Hz pitch dynamics (an inference). Declare structural failure if the solve diverges, if tip deflection exceeds about 25–30% of the semispan at launch dynamic pressure, or if any crease moment exceeds a fold-open threshold. The lock feature from the fold engine raises that threshold. Your added-mass term already exists, and it is needed: a flat plate's apparent mass is comparable to the plane's own 5 g.

Launch is the fourth gap, and the biggest one for distance. `design.js` fixes launch speed at **12 m/s for distance and 16 m/s for hang time**, with distance pitch capped at 30°. Measured casual throws are about 6 m/s ([Wooster 2021](https://physics.wooster.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/JuniorIS_2021_Miller.pdf)). Blackburn's record throw is estimated at **27 m/s** ([core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/267013002.pdf)), and Mach 5 was launched at about 40°. Kinetic energy at 27 m/s is about five times that at 12 m/s. Mach 5's jump from Suzanne's 69 m to 88 m with a speed design shows that the optimal airframe depends on the throw. Make launch speed a thrower-class parameter: casual 6–12 m/s, strong 15–20 m/s, elite 25–27 m/s. Within each class, co-optimize release angle over 0–45° for distance and 60–90° for duration, plus bank. Cap the usable speed with the launch-load structural check, so a flimsy glider cannot claim an athlete's throw. Simulate from release, or better from a 0.1–0.3 s wind-up, so that loop-outs at high speed are penalised automatically. Your `ceiling: 9` m also ends hang-time flights early. Record duration flights reach an apex of roughly 18 m in hangars, so treat ceiling height as a declared venue parameter. Otherwise the optimizer is solving a gym problem, not the record problem.

## Keep differential evolution, but replace the scoring wrapped around it

Your optimizer is not the bottleneck, and it is not optimal either. JADE-style current-to-pbest/1 is the ancestor of SHADE and L-SHADE, which won CEC 2014 and spawned the jSO and NL-SHADE winners ([CEC longitudinal analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24140)). At your budget of about 30k–300k evaluations per hour, population-based methods are the right class. Gaussian-process Bayesian optimization would be too slow, and SMAC-style surrogates only beat CMA-ES at around 10·D evaluations ([Hutter et al. 2013](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~kevinlb/papers/2013-SMAC-BBOB.pdf)). Two upgrades are cheap. Adding linear population-size reduction turns JADE into L-SHADE. Running BIPOP-CMA-ES as a second baseline adds a method that is strong on non-separable couplings such as CG against elevator against dihedral ([DEAP BIPOP](https://deap.readthedocs.io/en/master/examples/bipop_cmaes.html)). Plain CMA-ES stalls on integer genes because its variance collapses below the integer spacing, and margin-based variants fix that ([CMA-ESwM, arXiv 2205.13482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13482v2)). This becomes relevant once the genome gains categorical fold types and layer selectors.

The bigger structural change is to wrap the inner optimizer in **MAP-Elites** ([Mouret & Clune](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909)). Keep the best design in each cell of a grid indexed by features such as flight family (dart, glider or speed design), number of folds, aspect ratio and lock count. The reason is epistemic, not cosmetic. The simulator will be wrong somewhere, and a diverse portfolio means a single modelling error cannot invalidate every design you take to the hallway. Use TPE or SMAC only for small sub-problems, such as tuning the optimizer's own hyperparameters or choosing which designs to build next.

The scoring is where the current approach is clearly suboptimal. `evaluate.js` multiplies the robust score by three smooth penalty factors for flex, keel grip and static margin. Those are fixed penalty coefficients in disguise: they need tuning, they interact badly with noisy objectives, and they create flat regions where a design scores almost the same whether it barely violates or grossly violates a limit. Replace them with **Deb's feasibility rules** ([described here](https://aj.tubitak.gov.tr/elektrik/issues/elk-21-29-7/elk-29-7-24-2101-88.pdf)). Under these rules a feasible design beats an infeasible one, feasible designs compare on the objective, and infeasible designs compare on graded total violation. Switch to **ε-constrained selection** if the feasible region turns out to be thin ([Takahama & Sakai](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-68830-3_5)). Deb's rules also mean you never need to run the expensive simulation on a candidate that fails a cheap pre-check, and with about 50% of random samples infeasible that saves a great deal of budget. Static margin, launch-load deflection, keel grip and crash fraction then become explicit constraints with physical thresholds instead of exponents someone picked.

The ten scenarios use common random numbers, which is correct: every candidate sees the same draws, so rankings are consistent ([arXiv 2203.01996](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01996)). They fall short in three ways. They perturb one factor at a time, so the combinations that actually crash planes are never tested, such as a weak throw with an aft CG and a slightly over-bent elevator. They leave out the parts of the model you trust least: CD0, vortex-lift strength, crease rest angle and stiffness. And 0.6·mean + 0.4·min hinges on the single worst draw.

Jakobi's "envelope of noise" is the principled fix. Randomise exactly the quantities the simulator cannot be trusted on, so that evolved designs cannot depend on them. Jakobi found transfer to reality improved when the simulated noise was realistic ([Jakobi et al. 1995](https://cse-robotics.engr.tamu.edu/dshell/cs689/papers/jakobi95noise.pdf); [Jakobi 1997](https://www2.cs.sfu.ca/~vaughan/teaching/889/papers/jakobi_radical.pdf)), and domain randomisation is the modern form of the same idea ([Tobin et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.06907)). Concretely, draw 16–32 Latin-hypercube scenarios that jointly vary launch speed, angle, bank and body rates; fold placement (±1 mm, ±1°); crease rest-angle offsets of 5–15° and crease stiffness with a CV of 20–30%; CG shift; paper gsm; and multipliers on CD0, Kv and post-stall normal force. Keep the draws fixed within a generation and refresh them every N generations. Score with **CVaR at 20–25%**, the mean of the worst quarter of draws, which is more stable than a minimum ([arXiv 1509.05697](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.05697)). Re-audit the final elites on 500 or more fresh draws. Your existing nominal-scenario screen in `worker.js` is a sound multi-fidelity filter. Check its Spearman rank correlation against the full evaluation on a few thousand random feasible designs before relying on it to discard candidates, and replace the invented 0.5× score it assigns to screened candidates with a Deb-style "dominated" flag.

## Validate rank order with about twenty measured throws per design, not single best throws

Physical validation has to compare distributions. EPFL's robot folded and launched more than 500 planes from about 50 designs and found that **identical folds produced different flights**, clustering into nose-dive, glide and recovery-glide classes ([Sci. Rep. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-31395-0); [PopSci](https://www.popsci.com/technology/paper-airplane-robot-epfl/)). Fold stiffness alone varies widely from one specimen to the next ([Pradier et al.](https://hal.science/hal-01276578)). The evolutionary-robotics literature shows what happens when simulations are trusted blindly: top simulated solutions exploit badly modelled physics. Koos, Mouret & Doncieux fixed this by adding a learned "transferability" objective trained on only **about 10 real experiments** ([Koos et al. 2012](https://www.mn.uio.no/ifi/english/studies/masters-projects/robin/papers/evorob-koos-reality-gap.pdf)). PaperBot shows that real-world-only search works at tiny dimension, beating the best human design within 100 throws ([arXiv 2403.09566](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09566v1)). It cannot cover a 30–60-dimensional fold space, though, so in your setup the simulator searches and the hallway judges.

A workable protocol uses still indoor air, one weighed paper stock, and a folding template to bound fold error; the measured error then feeds back into the scenario noise model. Film each launch side-on in slow motion (120–240 fps) with a scale reference, and track the nose over the first frames to recover release speed and angle, as the Wooster study did with two cameras and Tracker ([Miller 2021](https://physics.wooster.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/JuniorIS_2021_Miller.pdf)). A rubber-band rail launcher removes most thrower variance when you want to isolate design effects. Use human throws only for the final thrower-class checks. Log a fixed set of trim adjustments as parameters so the simulation can be re-run in the as-flown state. For each design, record 15–20 valid throws with distance, time, lateral drift and EPFL-style behaviour class. Throw-to-throw CV is unknown and might be 20–40%, in which case 15 throws gives roughly a ±10–20% confidence interval; pilot data should set the real number. Run the measured release states through the simulator.

The primary metric is **Spearman rank correlation between simulated and measured performance across 5–10 portfolio designs drawn from different MAP-Elites cells**. Per-design bias is secondary, and Mann–Whitney or bootstrap intervals handle pairwise comparisons. Then close the loop. Fit CD0, the induced-drag factor, Kv and pitch-damping multipliers to the tracked trajectories by least squares on the longitudinal model; NASA's glide-angle method gives L/D directly from distance and height ([NASA GRC](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/WWW/k-12/VirtualAero/BottleRocket/airplane/glidprob.html)). Narrow the scenario multipliers to the fitted uncertainty. Add a transferability constraint learned from the sim-versus-real gap. Each round of about ten builds should tighten the model where the optimizer was pushing hardest. If rank correlation stays low after calibration, the simulator is missing physics, most likely flex or launch transients, and more optimization will not fix that.

## Conclusion

The leverage in this problem is not where it first appears. Darts and speed designs live in a regime where vortex lift and launch energy dominate, while gliders live where low-Re form drag dominates. Each regime breaks a different part of a standard vortex-lattice model, so the simulator's errors depend on which family the optimizer is exploring, and the optimizer will drift toward whichever regime the model flatters. That is the deeper reason for MAP-Elites portfolios, model-error randomisation and early rank-order validation: they keep the search honest across regimes rather than letting it settle into the one where the physics is weakest.

The no-tape rule turns fold mechanics into flight performance. Record planes use tape to hold layers together at 27 m/s. Without it, a plane survives a record-class throw only if its locks and layer stacks keep the paper closed, so the fold engine's layer-order and lock model directly limits how hard the design can be thrown. Two outcomes are realistic. The first is a pure-origami duration design that challenges Toda's 26.1 s fold-only benchmark. The second is a distance design that can be thrown at elite speed without opening up. Beating the taped, cut 31.2 s and 98.43 m records under stricter rules would be a stretch goal rather than the baseline expectation. Nobody has published the fold sequences of those records, and nobody has searched real fold sequences computationally, so this project could be the first to find out how close fold-only designs can get.
